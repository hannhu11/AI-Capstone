\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Automated verification of safety helmet compliance and helmet color classification on active construction sites is critical for preventing traumatic head injuries and enforcing zone-based role access (e.g., white helmets for engineers, yellow for general workers, blue for supervisors, red for safety inspectors, and bare heads indicating immediate compliance violations). Existing commercial monitoring systems frequently rely on manual Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) review or cloud-offloaded models that violate site bandwidth and privacy constraints, while many academic helmet detectors either collapse the task into binary presence detection or evaluate architectural modifications without rigorous data deduplication, controlled initialization audits, or production edge deployment.

This capstone project (\textbf{FA26AI16}, Group \textbf{GFA26AI17}) investigates a reproducible, end-to-end engineering and research framework for five-class safety helmet and color detection (\texttt{blue}, \texttt{white}, \texttt{yellow}, \texttt{red}, and \texttt{none}) on the \textbf{GDUT-HWD} benchmark, integrated into a containerized real-time Video Management System (\textbf{AEGIS-CCTV VMS Safety Suite}). First, we establish a forensically audited dataset pipeline that converts Pascal VOC XML annotations using exact 1-based inclusive geometry, detects and removes six SHA-256 RGB pixel-identical duplicates leaking between official \texttt{trainval} and \texttt{test} splits---preserving all 1,587 official test images while yielding 3,168 clean images (1,265 train, 316 validation, 1,587 test; 18,849 total bounding boxes)---and introduces a color-preserving offline hard-case augmentation policy (\texttt{hsv\_h = 0}, \texttt{hsv\_s = 0}) that expands Stage 2 training data to 2,530 images without distorting helmet chromaticity.

Second, we implement and forensically audit two published state-of-the-art helmet detection baselines under matched GDUT-HWD protocols: \textbf{Baseline 1} (\texttt{YOLO11n-DCRCF}, Zhao et al., 2025) and \textbf{Baseline 2} (\texttt{YOLOv8s-DWR-ASPP-NWD}, Song et al., 2024). Across both single-seed official test evaluations and five-seed matched validation audits (\texttt{seeds 3407--3411}, audited 1-to-1 across 16 public Kaggle notebooks), we demonstrate empirically that neither published baseline outperforms its matched stock YOLO counterpart on five-class GDUT-HWD: \texttt{YOLO11n-DCRCF} (\texttt{C3}: $57.283 \pm 0.173\%$ validation mAP@50--95; $54.37\%$ test) trails stock \texttt{YOLO11n} (\texttt{C0}: $58.812 \pm 0.082\%$) by $-1.529$ percentage points (pp) due to 73.78\% randomly initialized neck parameters and a P3 feature-level confidence drop responsible for 84 of 98 bare-head (\texttt{none}) false negatives, while \texttt{YOLOv8s-DWR-ASPP-NWD} (\texttt{V8F}: $60.781 \pm 0.353\%$ validation, exact 5-seed mean Precision $90.2447\% \approx 90.24 \pm 0.94\%$ under population SD \texttt{ddof=0}; $57.98\%$ test) trails stock \texttt{YOLOv8s} (\texttt{V8S}: $61.414 \pm 0.074\%$) by $-0.633$ pp while expanding model cost to 16.285M parameters and 41.23 GFLOPs.

Third, we conduct a controlled two-stage, seven-configuration ablation study (\texttt{A0--A6}) across both \texttt{YOLO11s} and \texttt{YOLOv8s}, evaluating \texttt{CoordConv} at the stem (\texttt{layer 0}), six stride-2 \texttt{RepConv} blocks across backbone and neck (\texttt{layers 1, 3, 5, 7, 17, 20} on \texttt{YOLO11s}), \texttt{BiFormerBlockLite} ($r=8$, $\text{topk}=4$, $h=4$, $C=512$) at P5 (\texttt{layer 10} replacing \texttt{C2PSA} on \texttt{YOLO11s}; \texttt{layer 9} appended after \texttt{SPPF} on \texttt{YOLOv8s}), and \texttt{Focal-EIoU} bounding-box loss. Reporting all metrics transparently, we observe that \textbf{\texttt{A1}} (stock backbone fine-tuned with color-preserving hard augmentation plus online \texttt{mixup = 0.1} and \texttt{hsv\_v = 0.45}) achieves the highest overall accuracy on both backbones (\texttt{YOLO11s}: $93.40\%$ val / $89.8\%$ test mAP@50 and $61.69\%$ val / $59.1\%$ test mAP@50--95; \texttt{YOLOv8s}: $93.14\%$ val / $88.7\%$ test mAP@50 and $61.19\%$ val / $57.8\%$ test mAP@50--95). The full \textbf{\texttt{A6}} architecture achieves $92.50\%$ val / $88.9\%$ test mAP@50 ($61.23\%$ val / $58.1\%$ test mAP@50--95) on \texttt{YOLO11s} and $92.56\%$ val / $88.4\%$ test mAP@50 ($60.68\%$ val / $57.4\%$ test mAP@50--95) with the highest validation precision ($93.21\%$) on \texttt{YOLOv8s}. Pre-finetune initialization audits isolate the root cause on \texttt{YOLO11s}: replacing pre-trained \texttt{C2PSA} with an identity-initialized \texttt{BiFormerBlockLite} lowers pre-finetune mAP@50 from $91.83\%$ to $86.34\%$ ($-5.49$ pp), whereas appending after \texttt{SPPF} on \texttt{YOLOv8s} preserves $91.88\%$.

Finally, we operationalize the GDUT 5-class detectors inside the \textbf{AEGIS-CCTV Enterprise VMS v2.6} platform, architected as both a 6-microservice Enterprise Docker stack (\texttt{frontend-nginx}, \texttt{api-gateway} with \texttt{HALF\_OPEN} single-probe Circuit Breaker, GPU \texttt{ai-inference}, \texttt{postgres:16}, \texttt{prometheus} exposing 20 runtime/cgroup metrics, and 15-panel \texttt{grafana}) and a Single-Node Edge profile. Key subsystems include a Dynamic Taxonomy Adapter (Helmet-Only mode), Class-Agnostic NMS (\texttt{agnostic\_nms=True}, IoU $0.45$) with separate bare-head threshold (\nolinkurl{NONE_CONF_THRESHOLD=0.25}), String-First color resolution, Confirm-Before-Mint non-biometric Upper-Torso Re-ID with Two-Stage Gate and 10-frame color hysteresis (\texttt{gdut\_reid.py}), monotonic \texttt{C960 Ultra} Binary WebSocket (\texttt{/ws/webcam}), Solo Focus locking with client-side vector rendering (\nolinkurl{DRAW_VIDEO_HUD=False}), instantaneous compliance KPIs, zero-downtime hot-swap APIs, symmetrical 8-frame violation debouncing with Telegram/Zalo alerts, an Image Audit modal, and Human-in-the-Loop feedback logging.
